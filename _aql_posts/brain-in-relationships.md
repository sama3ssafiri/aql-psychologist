How does it feel to be shut out by someone you care about? What changes when someone you trust stays close while you’re under stress? Choose a relationship experience, then select a region to explore findings from a related experiment.

## What the studies can tell us

The brain surface uses real cortical anatomy from the FreeSurfer fsaverage5 template. White glowing outlines show approximate anatomical regions discussed in the studies; they are not measured activation maps. Deep regions are visible through the surface. The hypothalamus is shown as an approximate location rather than an atlas segmentation. The research views represent different experiments, with different participants and comparisons. The outlines relate to selected findings from those experiments and are useful starting points for exploring the research.

The rejection view draws on a [meta-analysis of social rejection studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761131/), specifically the studies where participants relived rejection by a significant other. The comfort view draws on [a hand-holding study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201784/) that measured responses to the threat of electric shock. The regions outlined in the comfort view come from the association between higher marital quality and lower threat-related activity during spousal hand-holding.

Attachment is another way to ask how the same situation might be experienced differently. In [a separate fMRI study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39093338/), researchers compared how adult men classified into anxious, avoidant and secure groups responded to images depicting distress and comfort. The task and sample matter when interpreting the group differences.

The abandonment, uncertainty and distance examples link to [research on attachment anxiety during social exclusion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21467049/). They are everyday scenarios, not separately measured activation maps. Reassurance seeking and feeling secure use the related hand-holding experiment as context; those experiences were not directly measured in that study. The jealousy view draws on [a study of personally experienced infidelity and jealousy](https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1190085). The travelling lights are decorative signals, not anatomical connectivity.
