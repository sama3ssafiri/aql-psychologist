How does it feel to be shut out by someone you care about? What changes when someone you trust stays close while you’re under stress? Try each situation at a different felt intensity, then explore what researchers observed in a related experiment.

## What the studies can tell us

The brain surface uses real cortical anatomy from the FreeSurfer fsaverage5 template. White glowing outlines show approximate anatomical regions discussed in the studies; they are not measured activation maps. Deep regions are visible through the surface. The hypothalamus is shown as an approximate location rather than an atlas segmentation. The two views represent different experiments, with different participants and comparisons. The outlines relate to selected findings from those experiments and are useful starting points for exploring the research.

The rejection view draws on a [meta-analysis of social rejection studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761131/), specifically the studies where participants relived rejection by a significant other. The comfort view draws on [a hand-holding study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201784/) that measured responses to the threat of electric shock. The regions outlined in the comfort view come from the association between higher marital quality and lower threat-related activity during spousal hand-holding.

Attachment is another way to ask how the same situation might be experienced differently. In [a separate fMRI study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39093338/), researchers compared how adult men classified into anxious, avoidant and secure groups responded to images depicting distress and comfort. The task and sample matter when interpreting the group differences.
